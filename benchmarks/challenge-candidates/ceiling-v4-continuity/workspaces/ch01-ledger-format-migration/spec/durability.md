# Durable boundaries and immutable consumers

The protected `LedgerJournal` is the durable authority. It persists ordered raw v1 transactions, snapshot rows/cursor/version and the activation marker. Its `export()` creates a complete independent JSON image; `new LedgerJournal(image)` restores the same durable contents. Ledger instances and local state are disposable. Restart can follow any completed call, a thrown boundary callback, or a lost response. Previously successful effects survive; unaccepted effects do not appear. Every snapshot row and journal transaction remains tenant-local.

The deployed `legacyClient(journal)` reads raw transactions and appends valid raw v1 batches. It never invokes Ledger and cannot be altered. Its accepted writes remain visible to Ledger before, during and after migration, including updates to previously materialized rows and deletion followed by recreation. Conversely, a modern accepted write is immediately readable by this legacy client with exact decimal amounts and the same revision. The fixed `accountingScreen(ledger)` hides deleted rows but otherwise returns modern units and revision. Neither consumer may be modified by a submission.

A response may be lost by throwing a callback at `write:afterAppend`. Retrying the identical request after restoring a journal image must identify the accepted transaction, not produce another effect. A migration may stop before snapshot publication; no cursor or rows may be advanced by a failed publication. A later Ledger on the restored durable image must resume without access to any earlier Ledger object.

No requirement promises progress under infinite conflicting writes or endlessly injected crashes. With the finite interruptions below exhausted, repeated pages of a positive fixed limit catch up after at most ceil(remainingTransactions/limit) successful nonconflicting pages; activation can then succeed. Arbitrary memory faults, malicious malformed raw journal images and filesystem durability outside this model are out of scope.
