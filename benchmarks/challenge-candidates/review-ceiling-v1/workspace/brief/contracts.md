# Release contracts

The contracts below are jointly authoritative for the synthetic shared release. Legacy comments and a green CI summary are evidence to inspect, not overrides.

1. **Identity and ownership.** Every shared entity, cache entry, job, export and audit lookup is scoped to the organization and relevant resource. Identity is obtained from the provided synthetic session, not accepted merely because a caller supplied an object ID. Observer actions are read-only. Authorization must hold at each externally usable route, including retry/export/download.
2. **Human decision.** Only an explicitly approved revision may be published. Unknown, failed, stale or not-yet-reviewed evidence is not approval. Bulk actions apply to the user's currently visible and selected eligible items; filtering or navigation must not silently act on hidden items.
3. **Concurrency.** An edit based on a previous revision is rejected or resolved explicitly rather than silently overwriting a later edit. The check and state change must remain correct when requests overlap. Repeating a request after a lost response must not produce another provider side effect.
4. **Durability and recovery.** A job's visible terminal result is consistent with durable state and the independently committed provider effect. A restart or retry must not turn an unknown effect into a fresh attempt without reconciliation. Cancel/retry are scoped and state-aware. Audit records may not assert a success that was never verified.
5. **Legacy contracts.** Existing artifact IDs, review/fix states, finding dispositions, evidence references and revision relationships keep their meaning across import/export. A wrapper may support multiple versions but may not reinterpret a missing field as a favorable outcome. Fixed old consumers are part of the boundary, not disposable test fixtures.
6. **Partial and delayed data.** Separate the source of truth from derived views and caches. Later user intent wins over earlier outstanding requests. Partial failure preserves correct usable areas but clearly marks unavailable/old data and disables actions or summaries that depend on it. Zero, empty, loading, stale, unauthorized and error are distinct states.
7. **UI interaction.** The UI communicates scope, object, state and consequences before action. Mouse and keyboard can reach and leave dialogs/drawers; focus remains visible and returns predictably. Destructive actions do not become the unintentional default. Validation explains the field and recovery route and preserves safe user input. Narrow viewports and zoom do not remove essential information/actions.
8. **Architecture and libraries.** Domain meaning is not owned by UI adapters or remote transport DTOs. I/O, resource lifecycle, async cancellation, error normalization, schema/version translation and configuration each have accountable boundaries. Actual package/lock/provider contracts govern compatibility; do not invent a vulnerability from a version string. Dependency advice must identify a real compatibility/security/lifecycle consequence and a verification plan.
9. **Efficiency.** Cache and batching must preserve the contracts above. Large or repeated reads/writes are bounded; missing measurement is not performance success. Do not recommend global serialization or unbounded retries as a universal fix without showing impact on independent users and recovery.
10. **Review and verification.** Findings identify a causal condition and practical impact. Separate new regressions, pre-existing debt, explicit acceptable tradeoffs and evidence gaps. Group symptoms with a common cause rather than inflating issue counts. Propose a minimal adequate change, the consumers affected and a regression test; do not demand a particular design name without need. Record unresolved assumptions and a concrete way to decide them.
