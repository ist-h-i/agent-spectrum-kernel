# Dispatch, printer and shift monitor

A work order ships one accepted card revision to a non-idempotent external printer. `queue` takes site, id, slot and issue. For a matching current issue, accept the exact `read` packet at one revision, using stage ready, attempt 0. A missing or replaced issue returns `{"status":"retired"}`. Queueing again with the same site/id and slot/issue returns the original job even if the card has since changed or been replaced. Rebinding that site/id to a different slot/issue returns `{"status":"identity_conflict"}`. A successful job lookup or operation returns `{site,id,slot,issue,version,packet,stage,attempt}`. `job`, `dispatch` and `recover` take site and id. Unknown jobs return null. The kiosk monitor must agree with the desk throughout.

`dispatch` on ready begins the next positive attempt. Its packet is the originally accepted revision. While the attempt is outstanding it is uncertain, visible from another process. Two dispatchers must not submit the same attempt. Dispatch on uncertain or printed returns the stored job. The printer may stall before or after it issues a slip, be unreachable, or lose its caller to a crash. A printed response means the slip was issued. A sealed_empty response proves this attempt will never issue. On a channel error retain uncertain and return the job for operator follow-up.

`recover` on uncertain looks up precisely this site, job and attempt using the adapter. Printed becomes printed. Sealed_empty returns the job to ready, retaining the attempt number until the next explicit dispatch increments it. An unseen lookup or an unavailable channel leaves the result uncertain. Recovery, lookup and queue never send a slip. Attempts from a different site or an older attempt cannot settle the current one. A delayed send result must respect newer evidence and attempts, including if another dispatcher already finished. No desk, kiosk or recovery operation can require an external wait on another desk process's print or progress callback.

`context.send(site,id,attempt,packet)` is asynchronous and returns printed or sealed_empty or throws. `context.lookup(site,id,attempt)` returns printed, sealed_empty or unseen or throws. Repeated sends for the same attempt can issue multiple physical slips. The printer maintenance console accepts `{op:"close",site,id,attempt}` to close a physically empty attempt, `{op:"outage",site,on}` to change channel availability, and `{op:"slips",site}` for physical records. It is available to operators only. An empty slip list alone cannot close a pending attempt.

For exercises an operator can run a desk command with `DESK_HOLD=member`, `before-print` or `after-print`. At that callback it prints one JSON event on stdout and waits for a newline on stdin. Other processes operate normally. The operator may terminate the held process instead. These are real process boundaries, not application commands.

A supervisor sometimes requests a waybill after the driver has gone. `queueReceipt` takes site, job id in `id`, sheet ID in `sheet` and zero-based member position in `pos`. Its packet is the exact desk read at that applied or unchanged receipt, including the observed revision. New receipts remain printable after later edits, disputes, slot replacement and restart. A rechecked dispute that succeeds becomes printable at its successful revision. Other statuses, unknown sheets and out-of-range positions return `{"status":"unavailable"}`. Some installations have only an old receipt: its current card at the exact issue and revision, or the packet of any installed job for that site/slot/issue/revision, is acceptable evidence. Without evidence it is unavailable. An unavailable request reserves no job ID. Once the packet is known, an existing job with that exact packet is returned regardless of its stage. An existing ID bound to another packet returns `{"status":"identity_conflict"}`. Normal dispatch, recovery and kiosk monitoring apply to these jobs.

## Transferred flight books

The retired flight presses never reported individual outcomes to the new printer supplier. The installed archive kiosk accepts `fileBook` with `district,book,tickets,leaves,limits` and returns `{district,book,edition:1}`. Tickets list the original docket in filing order. Each is `{site,id,attempt,flight,depot}`, identifying one specific job attempt, its scheduled flight and receiving depot. A ticket belongs to exactly one book. A docket can include jobs held at a neighboring office or jobs yet to arrive in the installed jobs table.

There is one surviving leaf per physically issued waybill, and all paper attempts on the docket have closed. Each leaf belongs to one distinct ticket. A legible mark is a list of possible readings. `offices`, `flights` and `depots` on a leaf constrain the ticket's site, flight and depot respectively. All its legible marks must fit. An omitted mark is illegible and unrestricted. Each depot's `{depot,capacity}` in `limits` is the maximum number of leaves it could have received during that shift, including from other offices. Every ticket depot has one limit. Capacity can be zero or greater than the number of leaves. Several leaves with identical marks still represent separate physical slips. Regional books can contain hundreds of leaves with similar markings.

The desk operation `auditBook` takes `district,book` and optional `edition`. Omit edition for the latest. Unknown books or editions return null. The supervisor needs `{district,book,edition,status:"balanced",tickets:[{site,id,attempt,outcome},...]}` in original docket order. Compare all ways to account for the *entire* book. A ticket is `issued` if used in every complete account, `empty` if used in none, or `open` if accounts disagree. The identity of its individual leaf need not be known to establish issuance. If no complete account exists, return the same envelope with `status:"inconsistent",tickets:[]`.

A records clerk can correct a transcription with kiosk `amendBook`, passing district, book, current edition and complete replacement leaves and limits. The kiosk increments the edition. A stale edition is rejected. The original docket and earlier pages remain available through kiosk `inspectBook` (same address as audit). The archive may predate the desk upgrade or be amended between two desk calls. Audit is read-only, including for the job monitor. Clerks' corrections preserve conclusions already established by a balanced edition.

For a transferred uncertain attempt, `recover` uses the latest book for that exact site, job ID and attempt. An issued ticket settles the installed job as printed; an empty ticket returns it to ready. An open or inconsistent book leaves it uncertain, including when the new supplier has closed its own channel for that number. Ordinary recovery applies to attempts outside the docket. Only explicit dispatch can start a later attempt. The waybill's original packet and attempt number survive transfer. Once recovered as printed it can authorize relief freight across offices under the normal signed-allowance rules.
