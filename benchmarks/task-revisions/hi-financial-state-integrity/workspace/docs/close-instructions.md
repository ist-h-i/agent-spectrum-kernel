# Daily close and signed archive handover

Customers see daybook entries by posting date, while interest accounting uses value date. Each line returned by `statement(account, postingDay)` has exactly `ticket` (the teller's three-field object), `revision`, `valueDay` and `movement` (signed minor-unit string). The queried account and posting date are not repeated in the line.

A revision supersedes the previous distribution on its old value date and recognizes the new distribution on its new value date. Movements for the same account and value date are netted within the revision. A zero movement produces no line. Where the two value dates differ, enter the reversal before the replacement. The posting date for each movement is the later of its own value date and the current first open date. Turning the close never relocates an existing entry. A change of disposition can affect available cash without altering that day's posted cash.

A signed branch export has `format: 1`. Its balance and daybook tapes are different views of the same already-booked activity. `balances` are the current posted balances in major-unit decimal strings. `cards` list the branch's accepted orders, and `advice` holds every accepted ticket advice as tape rows. Tape exports can list advice out of revision order. A card's recorded `plan` is the tariff agreed at the branch, including grandfathered terms. The tariff register itself uses minor-unit limits. The archive's `lines` hold the already booked daybook in entry order. The archive example supplies every field shape. An empty book uses the same format with empty cards, advice, lines and feeds.

The archive carries each feed's signed high-water position in `feeds`. These positions are already accounted for. Individual wire terms are unavailable up to that boundary, so the receiver passes over that part of a redelivery without interpretation. Positions accepted by the live service do carry terms and must remain checkable across a save and reopen. Application snapshots returned by `dump()` can use any internal JSON layout. Previously issued saves, the input archive, and other books opened from them belong to independent callers.

## Books saved by the first shared service

The kiosk's `format: 2` save is still in circulation. `data/service-save.json` is an export from that application. At cutover the branch attests its `cash`, retained order summaries, `positions` counters, and booked `lines`. Open these as recorded, including lines booked by the previous service. A card's teller ticket is in the record. A legacy dictionary key is a kiosk storage address. The branch accepts new orders against the resulting available cash while outstanding cases await reconciliation.

These kiosks kept only the most recent revision number, gross and release disposition on each settled card. Their daybook cannot certify the value date or prior advice terms; a disposition or value-date change could have produced no lines. Feed counters have no signed per-row receipt or identifiable signed floor. For a page that actually overlaps such old positions, return `waiting` with the feed's durable position, without consuming further rows. An empty page at or before the counter is still an acknowledgment. Positions accepted by the repaired service have normal receipts.

At a fresh feed position, a purported repeat of the last kiosk advice contradicting its recorded disposition or independently determinable cumulative gross returns `conflict`. A tape movement for advice one starts at zero. The base for an old tape movement after advice one is unavailable. Agreement on the retained fields, or a repeat of an earlier unrecorded revision, leaves the advice unconfirmed and returns `waiting`. The bank needs its signed advice tape to resolve those cases.

A correction with a known cumulative amount outside the face is rejected. A valid correction following a positive kiosk carry waits because booking it needs the previous entitlement's certified value date. A zero-gross carry has no earlier cash distribution to reverse. Its next revision can settle normally against the retained gross and reserved balance. Cards with revision zero have no previous advice. These decisions and the existing readouts must survive another save and restart. Operations obtains a signed branch export if it needs historical facts beyond the kiosk record.
